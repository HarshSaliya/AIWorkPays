Which payout methods reach your country?

PayPal, Wise, Payoneer and direct transfer — which platforms use what, and where each one actually lands.

The rail matters more than the rate. A high advertised figure on a payout method that doesn't serve your country is worth nothing, and it is the most common reason people discover a problem only after they've done the work.

The rails you'll meet

  • PayPal. The most common default. Wide but not universal coverage, and receiving in a currency other than your account's usually incurs a conversion margin on top of the headline fee.
  • Wise. Generally the most transparent on conversion, with good coverage for cross-border payments. Requires you to hold an account.
  • Payoneer. Common where PayPal coverage is weak, particularly in parts of South and Southeast Asia and Africa. Watch the fixed withdrawal fee against small balances.
  • Direct bank transfer. Usually the cheapest when offered, but it is typically offered only in the platform's home market.
  • Specialist processors. Some newer platforms use payout providers built for gig payments, which often have better coverage in countries the incumbents skip.

Fees are not the whole cost

A fixed withdrawal fee behaves very differently against a $25 balance than a $250 one. Combined with a withdrawal minimum, a fixed fee can quietly take a meaningful percentage of your first payout. Percentage fees are more predictable but compound with conversion margin.

What to check before signing up

  1. Which methods the platform offers in your country, from its own help pages.
  2. The withdrawal minimum, and any hold period on approved earnings.
  3. Whether the fee is fixed or a percentage.
  4. Whether you already hold, or can open, an account on that rail.

If your country is poorly served

Filter the ledger by country, and favour platforms using specialist gig-payment processors over those defaulting to a single incumbent. The quiz weights country eligibility more heavily than any other factor for exactly this reason.
