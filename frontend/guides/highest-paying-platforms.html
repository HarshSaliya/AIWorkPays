Which platform pays the most?

The honest answer is 'it depends what you can prove'. Here's the advertised ceiling versus what most people actually see.

This is the most-asked question in AI work and the one with the most misleading answers, because the number everyone quotes is the top of a range rather than a typical rate.

Why the ceiling isn't the answer

A range like $25–125/hr is not a midpoint of $75. The top of that band is generally reserved for scarce specialisms — medicine, law, advanced maths — where the platform is paying for credentials it cannot easily source. General applicants land near the bottom.

The honest reframing: the highest-paying platform is the one that pays most for the thing you can prove. Ask what you can evidence, then find the platform that prices it highest.

Three ways the ceiling actually gets unlocked

  • A credential the platform is short of. Advanced degrees in specialist fields, priced accordingly.
  • Coding ability. The most consistent multiplier available without a specialist degree — coding queues routinely pay several times the general rate.
  • Passing a harder gate. Platforms screening with a video interview or a selective written assessment generally pay more, because fewer people get through.

What to compare instead of headline rates

  1. Effective rate. On task-based platforms, pay per task ÷ time actually taken.
  2. Work availability. A high rate on a platform with empty queues pays nothing.
  3. Time to first payout. See how long until you actually get paid.
